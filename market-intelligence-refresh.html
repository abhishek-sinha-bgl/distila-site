Illustrative workflow · Market & competitive intelligence

The market has moved.
Which conclusions should move with it?

A market study is useful when delivered. Its greater potential value may emerge later, when competitors act, regulation changes, new data appears and someone must decide whether an earlier market or segment assessment still holds.

Epistamate's foundational architecture is designed to preserve evidence and reasoning over time. A tailored market-intelligence workflow could use Frontier AI and an optional governed research corpus to direct attention to what changed, which prior hypotheses may be affected and the next set of useful insights or deliverables.

Beyond a one-off market report

Research is produced once.
Decisions are revisited repeatedly.

Marketing, strategy and business-development teams maintain target industries, segments, competitors and account priorities. A new search or model-generated market report can describe recent events—but rebuilding the reasoning behind last quarter's assessment takes additional time.

Existing assessment

Retain the baseline

Capture the market definition, segment attractiveness hypotheses, major assumptions, source evidence, uncertainties, alternatives and the human-approved conclusion.

New intelligence

Refresh selectively

Bring in relevant industry research, regulatory developments, competitor announcements and updated datasets. Preserve publication date, source roles and relevance.

Impact and attention

Revisit what matters

Identify which assessment objects may be strengthened, weakened, qualified or left unaffected. Investigate uncertainty before changing a strategic recommendation.

Illustrative quarterly refresh · fabricated example

New information need not
mean a new report from scratch.

Consider a strategy team maintaining an assessment of two hypothetical service-market segments.

Prior accepted position

Segment A was the priority

The team previously preferred Segment A because of a documented combination of demand evidence, customer access and implementation feasibility. Segment B carried unresolved regulatory assumptions.

New external developments

Two changes arrive

A new official requirement may affect Segment B's cost assumptions. Meanwhile, a competitor announces a new offering relevant to Segment A. Neither announcement proves a market-wide outcome.

Proposed impact

Two different follow-ups

Reassess Segment B's economics and legal applicability; check whether Segment A's differentiation hypothesis still holds. Other accepted findings may remain unchanged.

Possible next insight: “The existing preference for Segment A remains the starting position, but the competitive rationale requires renewed evidence; Segment B's economics should not be revised until applicability is established.” This is candidate analytical guidance, not an automated or validated investment recommendation.

Industry → segment → account

Reuse knowledge.
Revalidate its applicability.

An industry development may inform several market segments and accounts. Its source and provenance can be reused, but its relevance and decision authority cannot simply be inherited.

Industry evidence

One sourced development

A verified regulatory or technology development enters a maintained evidence corpus with identity, date, context and limitations.

Segment assessment

Different consequences

The development may matter greatly to one segment and little to another. The methodology and evidence needed to support that difference are explicit.

Account relevance

Not a client fact

A market signal can suggest a discovery question for an account team; it does not establish that a particular customer has adopted a technology or needs to buy a service.

The next set of useful insights or deliverables

Less reconstruction.
More informed decisions.

The professional may still produce familiar slides, spreadsheets, market briefs and account playbooks. The ambition is to prepare those from a maintained evidence-linked assessment rather than recreate the analytical chain every time.

Change brief

What actually moved?

A focused period-on-period note with relevant changes, unresolved signals and previously held assumptions worth testing.

Assessment refresh

What is affected?

Candidate revisions to segment attractiveness, competitive positioning, market-entry assumptions or strategic priorities, with disconfirming evidence retained.

Professional output

What goes into the next review?

A familiar management memo, market update, segment playbook or executive presentation—shaped by the team's existing framework and approval practices.

Trust and methods vary: official notifications, primary datasets, vendor announcements, analyst estimates and anecdotes play different evidence roles. Epistamate does not assign one universal trust score, or promote a provisional Frontier AI interpretation into an approved business recommendation.

Questions and boundaries

Designed around the method,
not another generic research feed.

Is Epistamate a ready-made market intelligence monitoring platform?

No. This page illustrates how the shared architecture could be tailored. General automated monitoring, recurring industry/segment/account refresh and enterprise integration are not released products.

How does this differ from asking Frontier AI for an updated market report?

Frontier AI is valuable for first-pass analysis and refreshes. The proposed distinction is maintaining explicit relationships among prior evidence, assumptions, accepted assessments, unresolved questions and new developments, then identifying which earlier conclusions may need review. That advantage requires workflow-specific validation.

Does a new competitor announcement automatically change the recommendation?

No. It may be a lead, not proof of competitive effect. The system could identify affected assumptions and propose investigation; the team retains authority to accept, reject or qualify a new assessment.

What would have to be tailored?

Market and segment taxonomy, source authority/trust tiers, evaluation framework, research permissions, refresh policy, attention thresholds, review posture and existing deliverable templates—all grounded in the organization's own method.
