The assurance gap

Governance tells you what should happen.
Assurance asks what your evidence actually establishes.

You may already have policies, named owners, control descriptions, human approval, evaluation scores and monitoring. Those are necessary foundations. They do not automatically establish that the controls work as management assumes, that the evidence measures the right outcome, or that an earlier validation still applies after the system changes.

Control present

Every answer has a citation

A source can be approved yet stale. A citation can exist without fully supporting the claim it appears beside.

Does the evidence establish current claim support—not just citation presence?

Control present

Every output is human reviewed

A recorded approval proves that a step occurred. It does not establish which material errors reviewers reliably detect under real operating conditions.

What has human review actually demonstrated?

Control present

An evaluator reports a quality score

A stable score can still be a poor proxy for the specific factual, policy or support failure management cares about.

Was the evaluator validated against the failure that matters?

Control present

The dashboard looks healthy

Monitoring can improve while the customer-facing outcome remains weakly observed, or while a failure path sits outside the signals being tracked.

What outcome is actually observed—and what could remain silent?

Research determines what deserves challenge. Evidence determines what can be claimed.

That separation is deliberate. A published failure mode is not a finding about your workflow. It is a reason to ask a sharper question, inspect the right evidence or design a more discriminating test.

Research that changes the review

Not research added as citations afterwards.
Research that changes what gets investigated.

GenAI control mechanisms are still being actively studied. Epistamate maintains research intelligence on those mechanisms and uses the facts of the workflow to decide which findings are worth bringing into the assurance case.

01 · VET

Start with bounded research claims

Relevant research is reduced to the claim actually supported: what was studied, under which conditions, with what limitations and what was not demonstrated.

02 · TRANSFER

Test applicability to the workflow

A finding from another domain does not automatically apply. The assessment compares mechanism, task, interface, reviewer role, data, dependencies and operating conditions.

03 · CHALLENGE

Use research to sharpen the evidence question

The relevant finding may expose a proxy, shared dependency or success condition that the client's existing evaluation did not test.

04 · ACT

Translate uncertainty into a proportionate next step

Where the evidence cannot resolve a material question, identify the smallest test, sample, validation or control strengthening likely to change the decision.

The unit of assurance

The unit of review is not the model.
It is the path from input to consequence.

A useful assurance conclusion needs a boundary. The same model can be acceptably controlled in one workflow and poorly controlled in another because the data, interfaces, people, escalation paths and consequences differ.

01InputQuestion, case, request or document
02ContextRetrieval, data, prompts and tools
03GenerationAnswer, recommendation or draft
04DecisionReview, action, send or escalation
05OperationMonitoring, incident and change

Workflow facts matter to research applicability. Whether a reviewer sees an automated score, whether the evaluator shares dependencies with the generator, whether retrieval can serve stale material, or whether a control is deterministic can change which research-informed vulnerabilities deserve investigation.

The engagement

From messy records to a bounded assurance case.
Without letting the model become the authority.

Client material may arrive as policies, logs, evaluation files, screenshots, incidents, dashboards and partial documentation. Structured tooling and language models can help reconstruct and analyse the case. Material conclusions remain evidence-linked and human-owned.

01

Define the decision and workflow boundary

Confirm the consequential workflow, intended use, material failure, current configuration, population, period and the management decision the assessment must inform. An initial engagement typically starts with one workflow so the assurance remains bounded.

02

Reconstruct claims, controls and evidence

Turn policies, architecture, logs, validation records, incidents and operating data into candidate workflow facts and management propositions. Every material fact remains traceable to its source; inferred facts are not silently promoted into the record.

03

Apply research-informed challenge

Map the workflow's control mechanisms to vetted research, test transferability and identify the few vulnerabilities or success conditions that materially change what should be inspected.

04

Assess what the evidence justifies

Separate control design from operation, operation from effectiveness, proxies from outcomes, historical evidence from current applicability, and hypotheses from demonstrated weaknesses.

05

Strengthen only where it matters

For material uncertainty, identify the smallest useful next evidence or test: a targeted sample, evaluator validation, source-support review, reviewer proficiency test, deterministic control, specialist validation or explicit risk decision.

06

Preserve the assurance baseline

Record the evidence, research state, configuration, assumptions and dependencies behind the conclusion so a later model, prompt, retrieval, interface or research change can be assessed against what was originally relied upon.

A one-off review can be strong and still become stale. Epistamate preserves what an assessment relied upon so a later change can be evaluated against the original assurance case rather than beginning again from a blank page.

Data handling

Your confidential evidence does not need to become
public-model context.

Some organisations cannot send workflow records, customer data, logs or internal controls to public cloud LLMs. The assessment is designed so public research intelligence can remain separate from confidential client evidence.

Model use is agreed during scoping

Client security requirements determine whether analysis uses local inference, a client-approved enterprise service, abstracted/de-identified inputs or manual review.

No confidential evidence is sent externally by default

External model use for client material requires an agreed path. The public research pipeline does not need the client's raw evidence to keep research intelligence current.

Source authority stays with the client record

Parsed text, summaries and model interpretations are derived working material. The original evidence and its provenance remain authoritative.

Why external review still matters

Strong AI can draft a strong review.
Trust comes from the process around it.

Language models can reconstruct controls, identify plausible weaknesses and produce excellent reports. They can also overstate evidence, change emphasis across prompts, disagree with one another and turn a plausible mechanism into a confident finding. Epistamate uses model capability without making model output the authority.

Grounded conclusions

Material client claims must connect back to the evidence actually supplied. Missing evidence remains missing; plausible interpretation does not become fact by repetition.

Named human judgement

Models can propose questions, mappings, research relevance, tests and prose. Material assessment judgement remains a human responsibility.

Deterministic safeguards

The structured assurance case preserves scope, lineage, versions, stale states and evidence relationships so later analysis cannot silently rewrite history.

Research that is itself challenged

Research claims are not accepted because an LLM says a paper found something. The claim, source support, limitations and applicability must survive verification.

The boundary

What this is.
And what it is not.

This is

  • A research-informed assurance assessment of consequential GenAI workflows
  • Evidence-led reasoning tied to the actual workflow, configuration and period
  • A distinction between demonstrated weakness, missing assurance and research-informed hypothesis
  • A precise route to the next evidence, test or strengthening action that matters
  • A preserved baseline that can support later reassessment

  • A regulatory-compliance determination
  • A generic organisation-wide AI maturity score

Common questions

Before you bring
a workflow.

What is GenAI Workflow Assurance?

It is a bounded, research-informed assessment of whether operational evidence supports the management claims relied upon about controls in a consequential GenAI workflow, and what proportionate next step would close material assurance gaps.

Why bring external research into a control assessment?

Because a control can exist and operate while still relying on an assumption that current research has shown to be fragile under some conditions. Research helps identify which assumptions deserve challenge and what evidence or test would discriminate them. It does not prove that the same failure occurred in your workflow.

What if our evidence is fragmented or incomplete?

That is common. The workflow and control picture can be reconstructed from policies, configurations, logs, evaluation files, samples, incidents, screenshots and interviews. Where effectiveness is not established, the review identifies the smallest additional evidence or test that would materially improve the decision.

Do we have to send confidential evidence to a public cloud LLM?

No. Data-handling and model-use arrangements are agreed during scoping. Depending on client requirements, analysis can use local inference, a client-approved enterprise service, abstracted or de-identified inputs, or manual review. Confidential client evidence is not sent to an external model without an agreed path.

What does the client receive?

An executive assurance brief, claim-level conclusions linked to evidence, research applicability findings, demonstrated weaknesses and assurance limitations, a prioritised evidence/test plan, an evidence and reasoning register, and a baseline for later reassessment.

Why start with one workflow?

The methodology can apply across consequential GenAI workflows. An initial engagement typically starts with one workflow because a bounded decision, current configuration and manageable evidence set make the first assurance case faster to understand and easier to defend. Additional workflows can be scoped separately.

Can we ask for a reassessment later?

Yes. The original conclusion remains tied to its workflow, configuration, evidence and research state. If the system or relevant research changes, the affected claims can be revisited against the preserved baseline rather than starting from scratch.

How long does it take and what does it cost?

Timing and fee are proposed after a short scoping conversation confirms the workflow boundary, evidence access, data-handling requirements and any specialist work. The aim is to keep the engagement proportionate to the decision and evidence involved.

Is this an audit, certification or compliance opinion?

No. It is a professional assurance assessment and decision-support engagement, not a statutory audit, certification, legal opinion, regulatory-compliance determination or general declaration that an AI system is safe or reliable.
