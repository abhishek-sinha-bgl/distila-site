The problem

Confident briefings
with unknown foundations.

An AI-generated briefing can sound authoritative and be entirely wrong. You have no way to know which findings are solid, which are contested, and which are one source repeating another. That's a governance problem, not just an accuracy problem.

Problem 01

No way to know what's solid

The briefing says "market growth is projected at 18%." One analyst's estimate? Consensus across five Tier 1 sources? Contested by a more recent dataset? Without knowing, the number is decoration, not intelligence.

Problem 02

Decisions made on snapshots that evaporate

The research that informed a strategic decision in Q1 is gone by Q3. When a board, regulator, or acquirer asks "what did you know?" — there's no answer. The EU AI Act and corporate governance trends are making this a legal exposure, not just a best-practice gap.

Problem 03

Briefings that bury the uncertainty

Generic AI tools produce fluent prose that smooths over contested findings. A risk that three sources disagree on becomes a confident-sounding single sentence. The executive who needs to know the uncertainty doesn't get it.

Problem 04

Intelligence that doesn't compound

Market monitoring, competitive intelligence, regulatory tracking — all producing findings that disappear after each session. Distilapp's knowledge graph keeps everything and connects it. The intelligence you gathered six months ago informs today's briefing.

How Distilapp works for you

Basic mode shows you the SNR score, the key findings, and the contested claims — without the pipeline detail. Advanced mode is there when you want to go deeper. The Decision Log is always on.

01

Basic mode — the briefing without the machinery

Switch to Basic mode and you see the SNR score (how good is the signal?), the top verified findings, the contested claims flagged explicitly, and the knowledge gaps. No pipeline controls, no provider toggles — just the intelligence. Advanced mode is one click away when you want to see the evidence behind a number.

02

Contested findings are labelled — not hidden

When two Tier 1 analysts disagree on a market projection, Distilapp tells you that directly in the output. "This finding is contested — McKinsey projects 18% growth, Oliver Wyman projects 9%. Both are Tier 2 sources. Confidence: 52%." You decide how to weight it. You're not surprised by it in the board room.

03

Board paper and executive brief — generated from evidence

The Publish flow generates a board paper or executive brief from the verified evidence base. Bottom line first. Risk flagged. Assumptions stated explicitly. The document is traceable — every assertion connects to a scored claim. Paste your organisation's template and Distilapp adapts the structure and tone.

04

Log the decision — record what you knew

When you act on the research, click Log Decision. Distilapp captures the full evidence state at that moment: every verified finding, every contested claim, every gap that remained. Timestamped, immutable, exportable. Six months later, when the board or a regulator asks what informed the decision, you have the complete answer.

05

Market theses that get tested over time

Stake a position — "AI adoption in Indian IT will drive 15%+ revenue growth through 2027" — and Distilapp tracks whether new evidence supports or challenges it across every research run. The knowledge graph accumulates. Your market intelligence compounds rather than evaporating between sessions.
