The problem

Confident briefings with unknown foundations.

An AI-generated briefing can sound authoritative and be entirely wrong. You have no way to know which findings are solid, which are contested, and which are one source repeating another. That's a governance problem, not just an accuracy problem.

Problem 01
No way to know what's solid
The briefing says "market growth is projected at 18%." One analyst's estimate? Consensus across five Tier 1 sources? Contested by a more recent dataset? Without knowing, the number is decoration, not intelligence.
Problem 02
Decisions made on snapshots that evaporate
The research that informed a strategic decision in Q1 is gone by Q3. When a board, regulator, or acquirer asks "what did you know?" — there's no answer. The EU AI Act and corporate governance trends are making this a legal exposure.
Problem 03
Uncertainty is hidden, not reported
The AI delivers a confident briefing. The uncertainty is buried. You make the decision not knowing that two of the five key findings are contested and one has no corroborating source. Distilapp shows you what it doesn't know.
Problem 04
No governance record for the AI's role
EU AI Act enforcement, regulatory scrutiny, and board governance expectations all point in the same direction: document what the AI contributed, how confident it was, and what you knew when you decided. No other tool provides this.
How it works for you

Briefing in. Decision on record.

On confidence and quality The brief is ready immediately. Below it, a clear signal: how many findings are weak, where sources conflict, whether the confidence is based on pipeline output alone or human-reviewed evidence. You decide how much to dig in. When you log the decision, the record captures exactly what had been reviewed and what was acknowledged as uncertain.
01

Bottom line first — uncertainty named

The Executive lens puts the verdict at the top: what the evidence says, the confidence level, the three things that matter, and the biggest risk to the assessment. Contested findings are flagged explicitly — not buried in footnotes.

02

You see what's solid and what's contested

Every finding carries its status: Verified (multiple independent sources agree), Contested (credible sources conflict), or Weak (insufficient evidence). The confidence score is evidence-weighted — not the model's self-assessment. You know the difference before you decide.

03

Decision Log — the governance record

When you act on a briefing, log the decision. Distilapp records every verified finding, every contested claim, every gap — timestamped and immutable. Six months later, when the board asks, you have the exact answer: what you knew, how confident you were, and what you acknowledged not knowing.

04

Same intelligence, different frames

Switch between Executive, Analyst, Financial, and other lenses to reframe the same evidence base for different audiences — board paper, investment committee, regulatory submission — without re-running research. The evidence is the same; the output format is your call.
