The common failure

Assertions, evidence, model reasoning and decisions
should not collapse into one polished answer.

01 · Claims are explicit

What exactly needs to be true?

A report, policy, control description, investment case or recommendation is decomposed into material propositions that can be individually supported, contradicted, qualified or left unresolved.

02 · Evidence has authority and provenance

A source existing is not the same as a claim being supported.

The method preserves original source identity, intake/acquisition context, exact support locations where available, source authority, warnings and version state. Lower-authority material can inform a decision without silently becoming stronger evidence.

03 · AI is advisory

Fluent reasoning is still candidate reasoning.

Frontier models can propose search lanes, classify, interpret, challenge and draft. Their output must be validated against the frozen evidence context, and model agreement never becomes independent verification.

04 · Applicability is explicit

Research relevance does not prove client-specific fact.

Published evidence can reveal failure modes and better tests. It does not prove that a particular workflow, control or business claim is true. Transferability is an explicit judgement, not an invisible prompt assumption.

05 · The narrowest supported conclusion wins

“Partly supported” is a valid result.

Uncertainty, contradictions and missing evidence remain visible. The system is designed to prefer the smallest conclusion the evidence warrants over rhetorical completeness.

06 · History is preserved

New evidence should update the case, not rewrite the past.

Claims, assessments, recommendations and outputs can become stale when their dependencies change. Reassessment creates a new current state against a preserved prior baseline.

Evidence → Research → Applicability → Priority → Test → Strengthen → Reassess → Stay current.
This is the shared reasoning pattern across research, workflow assurance and advisory decision work.

Why this is not another AI governance site

Governance can define the control.
Epistamate asks what the evidence lets you claim.

AI governance platforms are increasingly good at inventories, policies, risks, controls and monitoring. Assurance firms evaluate systems, governance and control effectiveness. Epistamate’s distinctive layer is narrower: the evidentiary chain underneath a consequential claim, workflow judgement or recommendation — and whether that chain remains defensible over time.

Research remains central

Use frontier intelligence to look harder.
Use Epistamate to decide what survived.

Plan and acquire

Question, intent and scope become a bounded acquisition plan. Strong external models can propose richer search lanes; researcher authorisation and explicit provider/network permissions stay separate.

Bind and challenge

Sources are evaluated for what they actually support. Secondary citation context does not silently become primary evidence. Adversarial challenge searches for counterevidence, qualification and applicability failures.

Review and continue

AI can prioritise the review queue, but researcher action is recorded separately from evidence strength. Gaps, contradictions and weak claims can drive Research Further without laundering old conclusions into new authority.

Advisory decision evidence

Working internal experiment

A materially different consulting profile has demonstrated evidence-room capture, versioned claims and recommendations, prospect dossiers, controlled pitch views, public-research import/review and report snapshots. It remains an internal/experimental pattern, not a finished client software product.

Long-term institutional knowledge: same-topic lineage and reassessment are real architectural capabilities, while durable cross-topic canonical Knowledge remains under rebuild. The target is provenance-rich reuse with recency and applicability checks — not simple retrieval of old text into a larger context window.
