EPISTAMATE · FACILITATOR BRIEFILLUSTRATIVE · NEXT SESSION

AI-agent discovery: who captures the value?

Making an offering understandable to customers' AI agents might increase its reach. But discoverability does not necessarily translate into revenue or margin for the manufacturer. Customers, competitors and intermediaries could gain more of the resulting value. The facilitator must keep these possibilities open and ask what evidence could distinguish them.

ORIGINAL POSSIBILITY · FICTIONAL PARTICIPANTS

Make our offerings discoverable to buyer agents.

The initial possibility was to describe product capabilities in a form that a customer's AI agent could interpret, rather than requiring the customer to browse a fixed catalogue.

Origin: constructed participant idea, not a group-approved opportunity or verified capability.

OUTSIDE-IN SCENARIO · FACILITATOR-INTRODUCED

Suppose an intermediary changes its ranking criteria.

Imagine a major AI shopping platform starts favouring machine-readable evidence of performance or safety when it recommends suppliers. Would our original idea still work, and what assumptions would change?

Status: hypothetical outside-in challenge. Not a verified platform policy.

A SHARPER QUESTION, NOT AN AGREED POSITION

Discoverability is not the same as economic advantage.

The original idea concerned a new way for customers to find products. The scenario raises another question: if an independent agent mediates discovery and comparison, who influences the customer's choices and who captures the resulting value?

Agent-facing product information might attract customers. It might also make suppliers easier to compare, reduce a manufacturer's pricing power or move part of its customer relationship towards an intermediary. None of these outcomes is established. A discussion would test them rather than assume that another channel creates advantage.

THE ECONOMIC VALUE LENS

Value created, captured or put at risk.

POSSIBLE VALUE CREATED

Better matching between customer needs and products, less discovery friction, additional buyers or opportunities for new services.

WHO COULD CAPTURE IT?

The manufacturer might win demand. Customers might gain bargaining power through easier comparison; agents or channel operators might gain fees, data or influence.

WHAT COULD ERODE IT?

Products may become easier to substitute, direct relationships may weaken, and the cost of participating in a channel could exceed additional revenue.

A management question: Would this serve an established priority such as customer reach, differentiation or margin protection? The fictional example does not presume that management has chosen among these objectives or committed resources.

CONDITIONS AND ALTERNATIVES

What would need to be true?

CUSTOMERS & CHANNELS

Would customers in the relevant market use these agents? Who controls recommendations, customer information, channel terms and intermediary fees?

PRODUCT & DIFFERENTIATION

Which performance, service or safety claims could be substantiated? Would meaningful differentiation survive easier comparison with rival offerings?

AUTHORITY & ALTERNATIVES

Who can authorise a transaction, and on what terms? Could existing digital channels or a narrower agent-assisted search deliver similar value without ceding the customer relationship?

The intermediary's importance, customer adoption, economics and transaction rights remain unknown. Agents might supplement existing channels rather than replace them.

A BOUNDED COMPARISON, NOT A BUSINESS CASE

What small investigation could change our view?

Choose one fictional product and a realistic customer requirement. Compare today's discovery journey with a hypothetical AI-agent alternative, using the same product facts and competing offerings. Examine which products are shown, who determines ranking, what the customer learns and whether the agent can recommend or is authorised to transact.

Ask whether the channel could plausibly change customer choice, acquisition costs, realised prices or the expense of participating. A result in which reach improves but manufacturer margin or control deteriorates is as important as a positive one. Neither outcome should be presumed.

THREE QUESTIONS FOR THE NEXT CONVERSATION

What would justify investing attention?

  1. Customer behaviour: Which customers could use agent-mediated product discovery, and what evidence would distinguish real demand from technical possibility?
  2. Creation versus capture: If the channel brings demand, who controls the relationship and economics? Could easier comparison or channel fees transfer margin elsewhere?
  3. Trade-offs and alternatives: What would make this worth exploring over direct channels, better products or simpler digital improvements? What evidence would lead us to continue, narrow or set the idea aside?
WHAT IS NOT ESTABLISHED

No verified marketplace ranking change, customer adoption, incremental revenue, manufacturer margin, transaction rights or sustained competitive advantage. No management priority, group agreement or investment authorisation is implied. These are questions a facilitator could bring back, not a recorded decision or a validated business case.

Why this example matters

A better question, not a premature investment decision.

A technical possibility becomes more meaningful when the discussion distinguishes who benefits from who can retain the economic value. An external development could create opportunities, erode an existing advantage or redistribute value even without the enterprise making a new AI investment itself.

The facilitator connects the original idea, the outside-in scenario, competing interpretations and the next discriminating questions. A group might later strengthen, narrow or set aside the possibility; this constructed example does not assert that any such judgement has occurred.
