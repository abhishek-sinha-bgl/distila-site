ORGANISATIONAL INTELLIGENCE · EXECUTIVE LEARNING

Imagine what could change.
Learn how to keep exploring.

AI is expanding what an organisation might do while changing the world around it. The Organisational Intelligence workshop brings a small cross-functional leadership group together to explore possibilities, consider their conditions and challenge assumptions across successive sessions.

A good starting point, not a prerequisite. Epistamate meets an organisation where it is. A team with a specific research, strategy, advisory or assurance need can start directly with that work.

An instructor-led experience tailored for one organisation, typically with five to eight cross-functional executives or senior leaders. Scope and format are agreed for the specific cohort.

What participants practise

More than brainstorming.
Thinking that carries forward.

Participants begin with ideas unconstrained by today's organisation, then examine what those ideas would require, what may challenge them and which questions merit reconsideration. The point is to learn an approach to organisational intelligence, not merely collect a list of AI use cases.

Imagine

Explore emerging products, services, relationships, organisational forms and ways of working without prescribing an answer.

Connect

Bring perspectives from different functions together while keeping direct contributions distinguishable from inferred relationships.

Challenge

Discuss enabling conditions, competing viewpoints, meaningful constraints and what remains uncertain.

Reconsider

Return to earlier thinking as new perspectives and selected external intelligence become available.

The guided experience

Four or five sessions.
One evolving conversation.

Across the longer programme, early sessions introduce the method and invite unconstrained possibilities. Subsequent sessions explore enabling conditions, practical challenges, adversarial questions and what changed in the group's understanding. A shorter four-hour intensive format is also being evaluated.

Private thinking

Participants can record reflections privately and explicitly choose what to contribute to the group's shared pool.

Collective exploration

Different professional perspectives are considered together without treating silence as consent or absent knowledge.

Reasoned recap

The facilitator can examine what changed and bring a small number of consequential challenges and open questions back to the group.

Personal learning

Planned learning reports reflect on the method and the participant's journey, not on an assumed organisational consensus.

Real participation is uneven. Missed sessions, incomplete contributions and late challenges should be accommodated rather than concealed.

Proportionate review

Keep imagination open.
Keep the relationships faithful.

Blue-sky thinking is not an academic evidence audit. A speculative proposition does not need an external paper to enter the discussion. The important safeguard is that an interpretation should not be attached to the wrong possibility, and the system should not invent an agreement, approval or established fact.

The Quick Pass approach provides a proportionate, provisional facilitator review for routine workshop synthesis, with deeper inspection when relationships are uncertain or consequential. A small number of consequential, unsupported assumptions can be highlighted as questions for later investigation rather than overwhelming the group with warnings.

The method is being refined through a facilitator-led prototype. Delivery arrangements, supporting intelligence and any integrations are scoped to the organisation rather than assumed to be an off-the-shelf, self-configuring service.

The learning promise

A repeatable method.
Not a guaranteed blueprint.

The intended outcome is that participants learn and practise a disciplined but exploratory way to think about future possibilities using collective intelligence, relevant information, constructive challenge and reconsideration. The programme intends to provide recap material and personalised learning reports.

It does not promise an approved AI strategy, organisation-of-the-future blueprint, breakthrough finding or predefined business result after four hours or five sessions.

Begin where the need is

Start with a workshop.
Or bring a specific question.

A facilitated workshop is a good introduction to the method. Research, advisory, strategy and assurance work can also be explored directly, using the standards that fit each task.
