The problem

Research that resets
is research that wastes.

Every policy inquiry spans months. Every session with a generic AI tool starts from zero. By the time you're writing the final brief, half your early findings are buried in folders, stale in chat histories, or simply forgotten.

Problem 01

Pre-publication material can't go to the cloud

Embargoed findings, unpublished datasets, working drafts — none of it should leave your institution's perimeter. Cloud-first tools create a compliance problem before you've written a word.

Problem 02

LLM confidence scores are meaningless

Research from 2025–2026 confirms that model-verbalized confidence is fundamentally broken — 90% stated confidence can mean 24% actual accuracy. Policy briefs built on uncalibrated confidence are liability, not intelligence.

Problem 03

Contradictions get smoothed, not surfaced

When two Tier 1 sources directly disagree, generic tools produce a paragraph that averages them. Distilapp flags the contradiction structurally — it's a first-class finding, not a footnote.

Problem 04

No audit trail for what the AI knew

A parliamentary committee or peer reviewer asks: what was the evidence basis for this recommendation? Generic tools have no answer. Distilapp's Decision Log records the full evidence state at the moment of every decision.

How Distilapp works for you

From inquiry to
defensible brief.

The same four-phase pipeline — configured for policy research terminology, pre-loaded with Tier 1 policy sources, and outputting in the formats parliamentary and institutional audiences actually use.

01

Pre-loaded with Tier 1 policy sources

Distilapp ships pre-configured with RAND, Brookings, CEEW, ORF, IMF working papers, UN research feeds, OECD data, and arXiv policy papers — refreshed daily by a local model at zero API cost. When you start an inquiry, you're not starting from a blank web search. You're starting from a curated corpus of institutional research, already scored by credibility tier.

02

Evidence-weighted confidence — not model opinion

Every finding is scored from source tier (+5 to +20), multi-provider consensus (+10 per agreeing provider), Socratic challenge survival (+10), document corroboration (+15), and recency decay. An OECD report corroborating a claim is weighted more heavily than a news article. The scoring is deterministic, stored on each claim, and fully transparent.

03

Contradictions surface — they don't disappear

When the IMF and World Bank disagree on a structural reform finding, Distilapp doesn't average them. It flags the contradiction with both sources named, the specific disagreement described, and a contested confidence penalty applied to both claims. Your brief can then address the disagreement explicitly — which is what peer reviewers and parliamentary researchers actually need.

04

Policy brief — in your institution's structure

The Publish flow generates a structured policy brief from your evidence base. Paste your institution's template or an example brief — Distilapp infers the section structure, heading style, and citation conventions and replicates them. Chatham House format, Brookings format, parliamentary brief, or your own custom structure. The output is defensible because every recommendation traces back to at least one verified finding.

05

Research Further — without resetting

When SNR or breadth is low, hit Research Further. Distilapp targets only the contested claims, unfilled gaps, and low-confidence findings — not the whole inquiry again. New findings merge into the existing graph. Sessions compound. The brief you write in month three is built on everything you learned in months one and two.

06

Decision Log — the audit trail regulators and reviewers need

When you finalise a policy position or publish a recommendation, log the decision. Distilapp captures every claim, its confidence score, the active contradictions, and the knowledge gaps at that moment. Timestamped, immutable, exportable. If a reviewer asks six months later what the evidence basis was, you have the exact answer.
